Framework Definitions & Methodology

This deliberately simple model explores how different assumptions change a result. It is not a scientific incidence study or a forecast.

1. Agent Population Tiers (N_agents)

  • Narrow Definition (10⁷ / 10 Million Core Agents): Hypothetical population representing highly autonomous, long-running systems. This includes advanced AI search engine crawlers, autonomous multi-step research bots, and heavy commercial deployment units with direct tool-use capabilities.
  • Broad Definition (10⁸ / 100 Million Agents): Hypothetical broader population including localized micro-agents, short-lived specialized sub-agents dispatched by primary systems, workflow automations, and lower-autonomy browser-automation script wrappers.

2. Distortion Factors

  • Hidden Multiplier (M_hidden): Adjusts for non-public incidents. The share of undisclosed events is unknown; the multiplier is not inferred from the databases linked below.
  • Assumed Lag (T_lag): The lag slider is a modeling assumption. Manifold measures time from first action to public disclosure, which may include both detection and reporting delays. Its 70-day median cannot be treated as a measured detection lag.

3. Incident Categorization

  • Agent-Incidents vs. Action-Incidents: A single agent executes thousands of individual operations daily. While Action-Incidents calculate the probability of failure per single API request or click, this model focuses on Agent-Incidents—a rough incident-count-to-population ratio. Without a defined time window and unique affected-agent counts, it is not the probability of an agent causing at least one incident.
  • Risk Profile: These events fall into the Low-Probability, High-Impact spectrum. Potentially severe outcomes are the focus; this tool does not establish their frequency.

4. Mathematical Formulas Utilized

To apply a hypothetical growth factor to an incident count:

I_scenario = I_known × M_hidden × (1 + g)^T_lag

To compute the modeled incidence against the active agent base (not annualized without a defined observation period):

Scenario Ratio = I_scenario / N_agents

5. Public incident sources (checked 31 October 2026)

  • Manifold — Agent Incident Ledger: 59 publicly disclosed incidents; 70-day median time to public disclosure among 43 cases with both dates. The ledger includes various agent failure types, not only confirmed high-impact harms.
  • Orca AI Incident Archive: 407 security-event records, of which 146 report a confirmed victim. Includes incidents, vulnerability disclosures, research demonstrations and other event types.
  • StupidLLM: 140 documented AI coding-agent failures of varying severity; not a global high-impact incident count.
  • OECD AI Incidents Monitor: broader AI incidents and hazards, not specific to autonomous agents.

Important: These collections overlap, use different inclusion criteria, and do not establish the number of unreported incidents. Their totals must not be added together. The 59-case option is a broader-source scenario, not a verified count of severe incidents.

Model limitation: Multiplying cumulative observed incidents by a growth factor is a heuristic, not a statistically derived correction for reporting lag. An actual incidence rate would require a consistent incident definition, observation period, exposure denominator and reporting model.
